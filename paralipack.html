Why this matters here

A quick field clearance.
A cost beyond the field.

Stubble burning is a recurring issue in northern India, particularly in the paddy–wheat farming regions of Punjab and Haryana. A short gap between crops and the cost of handling straw make alternatives difficult to adopt.

01 / Air

Smoke does not stay on the farm.

Burning releases particulate pollution and gases into the air. Seasonal crop fires add to regional air pollution alongside traffic, industry, dust, and other sources.

02 / Soil

Nutrients leave with the smoke.

Burning loses organic matter and nutrients that could otherwise support the soil. Keeping suitable residue on the land is also a valuable option.

03 / Value

A useful raw material is lost.

Straw contains fibres that can be processed for paper and packaging. Burning ends that opportunity before a buyer can use the material.

The solution has to work for farmers. A no-burning message is not enough. Timely pickup, nearby machinery, clear quality standards, and an agreed payment matter. This project proposes a use for surplus collectable straw; it does not assume every field should have all its residue removed.

The ParaliPack idea

From harvest leftovers
to something useful.

Connect farmers and local collection groups with a qualified fibre-processing and packaging partner. Start with a small, tested non-food packaging product.

STEP 01

Collect & bale

Agree on suitable surplus straw, arrange pickup after harvest, and keep bales dry and free from dirt.

STEP 02

Prepare the fibre

A processing partner cleans and prepares the straw, then uses a suitable pulping process. Rice straw’s silica content needs process-specific handling.

STEP 03

Shape the packaging

Form pulp into protective inserts, or make board and convert it into boxes. Other fibres or additives may be needed for strength.

STEP 04

Test & sell

Dry, trim, and test the product for its intended load and use. Sell only after buyers approve samples, performance, and price.

What could we make?

Start with packaging
people already need.

These are product concepts to prototype with a manufacturing partner. They are not finished or certified ParaliPack products.

Concept 01 · Board route

Shipping & gift boxes

Straw-fibre board, potentially blended with other fibres, for small retail packs and gift boxes. Confirm strength, moisture resistance, and actual straw content.

Concept 02 · Moulded fibre

Protective inserts

Custom shapes to hold small products inside a box. Begin with one local buyer’s item and test fit, cushioning, and drop performance.

Concept 03 · Mycelium route

Corner protectors

Crop-residue cushioning inspired by ICAR-CIPHET’s biothermocol technology. Licensing, production conditions, and buyer testing need to be checked.

Straw-based does not automatically mean plastic-free, food-safe, recyclable, or home-compostable. Those claims depend on the complete formulation, coatings, tests, and available disposal systems.

Make the chain worth participating in

Who earns—and how?

The opportunity comes from delivering a usable material and a product that buyers reorder. Each participant has different costs and risks.

The farmer

Receives an agreed payment for acceptable straw. Any collection or handling costs the farmer pays must be deducted to find their net income.

The collection group

An FPO or local entrepreneur earns a collection or supply margin, covering baling, transport, storage, and seasonal losses.

The packaging business

Sells tested packaging to retailers or manufacturers. Profit depends on yield, rejection rates, production cost, and confirmed orders.

A practical starting point: arrange a small trial with an existing processor and one packaging buyer before considering a factory. Government supply-chain programmes have used agreements between aggregators and straw-using industries.

Questions worth asking

Before calling it a solution.

Is packaging the best use for all stubble?

No single use fits every location. In-field residue management, composting, energy, and other uses can also be appropriate. Packaging is this project’s chosen opportunity; local soil needs, transport distances, processing capacity, and demand decide whether it makes sense.

Can we just press raw straw into a strong shipping box?

Not reliably. Fibre preparation, binding or pulping, drying, and product design determine performance. A commercial box may need blended fibres and coatings. The simple drawings on this page show product ideas, not proven manufacturing specifications.

Does this automatically reduce environmental impact?

Preventing an open burn avoids that burning event, but packaging production also uses energy, water, and transport. Compare the full process and end-of-life route before claiming a carbon saving. This project does not claim measured emissions reductions.

Could these be used as food boxes?

That would require appropriate food-contact testing and compliance for the full material, including any coating, ink, or adhesive. Our suggested first pilot is non-food packaging.
